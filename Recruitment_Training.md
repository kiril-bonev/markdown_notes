# Recruitement training

## Hiring criteria

Avoid using wordings such as 'A-Player', 'Passionate', etc. Focus instead on evidence-based hiring criteria, such as:

- Skills
- Behaviours
- Intrinsic motivators
- Achievements

> The more must-haves, the smaller your talent pool.

> Too often, the job description wishlist becomes a must-have list and it's just not realistic.

> You don't expect each person (KB: from the interview team) to cover everything. When you do that, you end up rushing, you get only surface-level responses and you risk letting the good talkers impress you versus hiring the good performers.

> What we focus on as interviewers during an interview tells the candidate what we value. So another reason to make sure you don't just run a candidate through a purely technical or skill-based interview is you might be communicating that we don't care that they're not collaborative or we don't value collaboration or that we don't even care about their past experience 'cause all we do is have them do a technical test.

### Good vs. Bad Hiring Criteria

> So, bad hiring criteria would be something like a top-rated university degree or worked only at a cool company or 10 years of experience, or, you know, firm handshake.

> Good would be a success selling enterprise software to $100 million size companies in Asia.

*KB: However, I still believe that top-rated university sends a signal of someone who puts value into their future choices and someone who is inclined to walt the extra mile. Same for the company background. We cannot expect someone just after university to have sold 100 mio. USD software in Asia, and we should not disqualify based on this...*

### Who to be part of the interviewing team

- Should manager-level candidates get interviewed by their future or potential direct reports?

> In some companies, if the manager job is opening and there are three people that would potentially report to this manager, you can't have them pick their own manager. Other companies would think it'd be crazy not to include them because they wanna value those employees, they want the buy-in of those employees to help select their new boss. Now, they don't get to choose. It's ultimately, you know, the manager's manager who's deciding who we hire, but it depends a lot on the culture. 

- Should the hiring manager's manager be part of every interview process?

 > For critical roles, typically, I say, yes to maybe. And sometimes they're on there not so much to interview, but just to help sell or close the candidate. 

 - Should HR recruiting be part of every on-site interview or every final Zoom interview or virtual interview?

> Many recruiting teams are too busy sourcing, screening, scheduling, and closing to do full 60-minute on-site or final interviews. So this is like, yeah, it'd be nice, but a lot of organizations we work with, they just don't have time to do a full behavioral interview.
